Your rights and obligations in Colombia › Hosts and short-term rentals

A guest won’t leave or breaks the rules: can I call the police?

What the law says, the deadlines and where to complain. Then get free guidance in 2 minutes.

Reference translation of Colombian rules: the Spanish legal text prevails.

Information updated on 8 November 2026.

Deadlines that matter

  • Complaint to the police inspector as the lawful holder: file it as soon as possible; it lapses after 4 months (Law 1801 of 2016, arts. 79 and 80). The 48-hour preventive action (art. 81) is meant for unlawful occupations: using it against a guest who entered under a contract is debatable.
  • Police protection of occupation: expires after 4 months (Law 1801 of 2016, art. 80).
  • Report to the platform: the same day, through its emergency channel.

What the law says

  • Accommodation ends when the term expires, for non-payment, breach of the official accommodation regulations or another cause expressly agreed (Commercial Code, arts. 1193 and 1197). Your house rules only count as a cause if the guest expressly accepted them when booking.
  • If you end the contract, make an inventory of the guest’s belongings in front of witnesses before removing them (Commercial Code, art. 1198). If the guest doesn’t pay, the law provides for an auction through an authorised auctioneer, not keeping the items (art. 1199).
  • While the stay is ongoing, the accommodation is the guest’s home (Constitution, art. 28; C-519 of 2007): entering without permission or recording their private life inside can be a crime (Criminal Code, art. 189).
  • For noise, parties or fights, the police can intervene under the coexistence rules (Law 1801 of 2016, art. 33).
  • If the guest stays after the booking ends, the contract has ended (Commercial Code, art. 1197). Accommodation has its own mechanism, different from lease repossession: an inventory in front of witnesses and removal of belongings (art. 1198), and a complaint to the police or inspector as the lawful holder (Law 1801 of 2016, arts. 77 and 79). Never by force.

Where and how to complain

  1. Write to the guest through the platform with the reason and check-out time, citing your house rules.
  2. Alert the platform through its emergency or safety channel.
  3. If there is noise, a fight or danger, call 123; if the guest stays after the booking ends, ask the police or police inspector for protection.
  4. Don’t go in, don’t change the lock and don’t remove the guest’s belongings without an inventory in front of witnesses.
  5. If the guest didn’t pay or caused damage, use the platform’s claim process and, if that fails, a civil action.

Documents to gather

  • Booking with dates and accepted house rules
  • Messages with the guest and the platform
  • Photos or video of damage or noise, taken from common areas
  • Inventory signed in front of witnesses, if belongings were removed
  • Reference number of the report to the police or platform
