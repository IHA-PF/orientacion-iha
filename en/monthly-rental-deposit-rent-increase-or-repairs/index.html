Your rights and obligations in Colombia › Renting and housing

Monthly rental: deposit, rent increase or repairs

What the law says, the deadlines and where to complain. Then get free guidance in 2 minutes.

Information updated on 8 November 2026.

Deadlines that matter

  • Early termination requires written notice: check the period in your contract and in Law 820.

What the law says

  • A stay of 30 days or more in urban housing is usually governed by Law 820 of 2003, not by tourism rules.
  • The landlord cannot require cash deposits or other collateral to secure payment (Law 820 of 2003, art. 16).
  • Rent can only rise once every 12 months and by no more than the previous year’s consumer price index (Law 820 of 2003, art. 20).
  • The landlord must hand over the property in good condition and make necessary repairs; the tenant is responsible for minor repairs caused by their use (Law 820 of 2003, arts. 8 and 9).

Where and how to complain

  1. Complain in writing to the landlord or the agency.
  2. Try conciliation.
  3. If there’s no agreement, the case goes to a civil judge; municipalities oversee rental agencies (in Bogotá, the Housing Secretariat).

Documents to gather

  • Lease contract
  • Rent payment receipts
  • Inventory and handover photos
