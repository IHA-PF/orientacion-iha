I was scammed through a transfer (Bre-B, keys or instant transfer)

What the law says, the deadlines and where to complain. Then get free guidance in 2 minutes.

Reference translation of Colombian rules: the Spanish legal text prevails.

Information updated on 8 November 2026.

Deadlines that matter

  • Report to the bank: immediately, within the first hour.
  • Lawsuit before the Financial Superintendence: up to one year after the relationship with the bank ends.

What the law says

  • Your bank has a heightened duty of security: it must identify you properly and watch for unusual transactions by amount, frequency or destination.
  • If the bank failed to detect or stop clearly unusual transactions, the Financial Superintendence can order it to return all or part of the money; it also checks whether you acted carefully, for example whether you shared passwords or reported late.
  • If you authorised the transfer yourself because you were deceived, getting the money back is harder: that is why reporting immediately and filing a police report are decisive.
  • You can complain to the Financial Consumer Ombudsman or the Financial Superintendence, and sue before the Financial Superintendence without a lawyer if the amount is below 40 minimum monthly wages.

Where and how to complain

  1. Call your bank on its official line, report the transfer and block the app and cards. Some banks let you report it from their app.
  2. Ask the bank in writing to contact the receiving bank to try to hold the funds, and keep the reference number.
  3. File a report with the Attorney General’s Office or the Police’s virtual CAI.
  4. If the bank doesn’t answer or refuses, go to the Financial Consumer Ombudsman and the Financial Superintendence.

Documents to gather

  • Transfer receipt: time, amount and destination key or account
  • Screenshots of the chat, link or call used in the scam
  • Reference number of the report to the bank
  • Police report
