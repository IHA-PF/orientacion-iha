Information updated on 19 November 2026.

Deadlines that matter

  • Do these checks before paying a reservation deposit or signing the purchase agreement or the lease.
  • Permit applications filed in due form before the new POT takes effect are decided under the rules in force when they were filed, unless the applicant asks for the new ones (Decree 1077 of 2015, art. 2.2.6.1.2.1.2, paragraph).

What the law says

  • “Airbnb-ready” is not enough. To rent by the night (stays under 30 days: Law 300 of 1996, art. 79) in Medellín you need all of these: land use that allows it, building rules that expressly authorise it, a building permit with a compatible use, and the RNT before you start.
  • If the building rules only allow stays of 30 days or more, you won’t be able to rent by the night, whatever the sale listing says. Ask the seller where the building rules allow stays under 30 days, and ask for the document.
  • The new POT, approved by the City Council on 5 November 2026 and awaiting signature and publication, would ban short-term rentals in low-mixed-use zones, limit them in medium-mixed-use zones to areas near mass transit such as the metro, and allow them in high-mixed-use zones: about 10% of urban land. Until it is published, Agreement 48 of 2014 applies.
  • The protection for activities already established (art. 331 of the draft) requires having operated lawfully and without interruption. If you buy a property that operated without an RNT or in a zone where it wasn’t allowed, that protection won’t help you. It is also not yet clear whether it survives a change of owner: the final text will say.
  • If you plan to rent an apartment to sublet it by the night, you also need the owner’s written authorisation (Law 820 of 2003, art. 17), and the same zoning and building rules apply.
  • If a developer or real estate agency sold you the property as “Airbnb-ready” and it isn’t, keep the listing: it may be misleading advertising (Law 1480 of 2011, art. 30).

Where and how to complain

  1. Land use: look up the property’s address or CBML code on the Medellín mayor’s office portal and ask the Planning office for a written statement.
  2. Building rules and minimum stay: a certificate from the building administrator and the deed of the building rules.
  3. Building permit: the urban permit office (curaduría) that issued it.

Documents to gather

  • Property title certificate
  • Full building rules and minutes of recent assemblies
  • Administrator’s certificate on short-term rentals and minimum stay
  • Building permit and land-use statement
  • If the seller already rented short-term: RNT and FONTUR returns
