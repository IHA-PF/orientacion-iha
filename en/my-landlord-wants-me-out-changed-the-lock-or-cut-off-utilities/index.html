Your rights and obligations in Colombia › Renting and housing

My landlord wants me out, changed the lock or cut off utilities

What the law says, the deadlines and where to complain. Then get free guidance in 2 minutes.

Reference translation of Colombian rules: the Spanish legal text prevails.

Information updated on 8 November 2026.

Deadlines that matter

  • Complaint to the police inspector if you are kept out: file it as soon as possible; it lapses 4 months after the event (Law 1801 of 2016, art. 80).
  • Landlord’s notice to end the lease without fault on your part: at least 3 months (Law 820 of 2003, art. 22).
  • If there is a repossession case, keep paying the rent: if you don’t deposit it, you won’t be heard in the case (General Code of Procedure, art. 384).

What the law says

  • If you don’t hand back the home, the landlord must bring a repossession case before a judge (General Code of Procedure, art. 384). Neither the landlord nor the police can replace that order.
  • Without fault on your part, the landlord can end the lease: during renewals, with at least three months’ written notice by certified mail and compensation of three months’ rent (Law 820 of 2003, art. 22(7)); or at expiry, with three months’ notice, to live in, demolish or repair, or sell the property, and after four years of lease also at will, with compensation of one and a half months’ rent (art. 22(8)).
  • If the landlord deliberately cuts off your utilities, you can end the lease and pay for reconnection, deducting it from the rent (Law 820 of 2003, art. 24(1)).
  • If you are kept out, for example by a changed lock: as a tenant you are a lawful holder (Civil Code, art. 775) and can file a complaint with the police inspector, who can order your occupation restored (Law 1801 of 2016, arts. 77(5) and 79). At the time, call 123.
  • Entering your home without permission, even as the owner, can be violation of another’s dwelling (Criminal Code, art. 189); forcing you out with threats can be unlawful coercion (art. 182).
  • Temporary measure: if your home or lease was directly affected by the earthquake of 10 August 2026, Decree 1423 of 2026 suspends enforcement of repossession orders for 6 months, postpones rent increases and lets leases ending between 10 August and 31 December 2026 be extended by 6 months, with exceptions (risk of collapse, landlord’s own housing).

Where and how to complain

  1. Don’t hand over the keys or sign a handover under pressure.
  2. Ask for the reason and the legal ground in writing.
  3. If the lock is changed or you are kept out, call 123 or go to the police inspector.
  4. Keep paying the rent and keep the receipts; if the landlord refuses it, ask us how to deposit it.
  5. If utilities were cut, keep the reconnection receipts.

Documents to gather

  • Lease contract
  • Rent payment receipts
  • Notices or letters from the landlord
  • Photos or video of the changed lock or the cut-off
  • Police report number
