How to gather and protect your evidence

Evidence turns your account into a fact that the company, the SIC (Colombia’s consumer authority) or a judge can check. Here you will see what to gather for your case, how to store it so it keeps its value, and how to protect your data when you share it.

Reference translation of Colombian rules: the Spanish legal text prevails.

Information updated on 9 November 2026.

What evidence is worth

Whoever states a fact must prove it (Colombian General Code of Procedure, art. 167). In a complaint, your evidence forces the company to answer on concrete facts rather than your word against theirs. Evidence is worth three things: it has not been altered, it has a date, and it can be tied to the company (its name, its number, its website).

  • Documents and messages in their original formatStrongest
    The invoice, the contract, the email saved as a file, the chat exported from the app, the bank statement. The law accepts them as evidence and weighs them by how they were created and stored, and whether they remain intact (Colombian Law 527 of 1999, arts. 10 and 11; General Code of Procedure, arts. 243 and 247).
  • Photos, videos, screenshots and witnessesUseful
    Dated photos and videos, full screenshots, the name and contact details of anyone who saw what happened. They support your account, especially when they match each other.
  • A printed screenshot without the originalWeak on its own
    Colombia’s Constitutional Court treated printed WhatsApp screenshots as mere circumstantial evidence (Judgment T-043 of 2020), although other authorities treat them as documents. So, besides the screenshot, keep the original on your phone or exported.

Seven rules for gathering it

  1. Today, not tomorrow: listings change, chats get deleted and some deadlines are 5 business days.
  2. With the date and time visible: in the screenshot, in the file name, or by saying the date out loud at the start of a video.
  3. The original as well as the screenshot: export the chat from the app, save the email as a file and download the invoice from the company’s website.
  4. Unedited: do not crop, mark or filter the original. If you need to highlight something, do it on a copy.
  5. In writing: if you spoke by phone, confirm what was said by email or message and note the reference number.
  6. The whole listing: with the web address, price, conditions and date. If you can, also save it as a PDF from your browser (Print, Save as PDF).
  7. Two copies: one on your phone and one in the cloud or on a computer, in one folder per case.

What to gather for your case

Accommodation and tourism (guests)

  • The listing as it was when you booked: photos, description, price and cancellation policy.
  • The booking confirmation and proof of payment.
  • Messages with the host, hotel or agency, and with the platform, inside the app.
  • Photos and video of the place on arrival and departure, with the date visible.
  • The complaint number the platform gives you.
  • Receipts for anything extra you paid: another place to stay, transport, meals.

Flights and transport

  • The ticket, booking and boarding pass.
  • The cancellation or delay notice, with the time it arrived.
  • Receipts for the meals, hotel and transport you had to pay for.
  • Baggage: the irregularity report made at the airport, the tag, photos of the damage and a list of the contents, with receipts if you have them.
  • Ride apps and taxis: the trip receipt, the route and a screenshot of the estimated fare.
  • Your written complaint and its reference number.

Online purchases and products

  • The offer or advert, with date, price and conditions.
  • The order, invoice and proof of payment.
  • The shipment tracking and the promised dates.
  • Photos or video when opening the parcel and of the defect.
  • The warranty and the repair orders for each visit.
  • Messages with the seller.

Banks, payments and charges

  • The statement showing the charge you do not recognise.
  • The reference number and time of your report to the bank.
  • The payment reversal request and its answer.
  • The police report, if there was fraud.
  • Screenshots of suspicious messages or emails, without opening their links.

Phone, internet and utilities

  • The contract or the terms you accepted.
  • The bills with the charge you dispute.
  • The CUN or reference number of each complaint.
  • Speed tests or screenshots of the outages, with dates.
  • Photos of the meter.
  • Proof of your cancellation request.

Digital platforms: account, payouts and listings

  • The copy of your data the platform lets you download (in the account settings it is often called “Download your information”).
  • The suspension, removal or withholding notices and emails, with dates.
  • Your payout history and pending balance.
  • The terms and policies in force on the date of the problem, saved as PDF.
  • Screenshots of your profile, ratings and listings before they disappear.
  • Your appeal and the answer.

Hosts and investors

  • The valid RNT (National Tourism Registry) of each unit and its certificate.
  • The building rules or the administrator’s certificate.
  • The TRA guest records and the SIRE reports of foreign guests.
  • The liability insurance policy.
  • Check-in and check-out photos and inventory for each guest.
  • Messages with guests and with the platform inside the app, and damage reports made on time.
  • Repair invoices.

Health and personal data

  • The medical order and the answer from your health insurer (EPS) or plan.
  • The reference number of your complaint (PQRS).
  • Screenshots of marketing messages or calls you did not authorise, with date and number.
  • Proof of your request or complaint about your data.

How to keep it digitally

  • WhatsApp: export the chat with its media (on Android: menu, More, Export chat; on iPhone: tap the contact’s name, Export chat). Do not delete the chat from your phone: the original is the best evidence.
  • Email: save the message as a file (Download message or Save as) or print it to PDF with the sender, date and subject visible.
  • Web pages and listings: a full screenshot showing the web address and the date, plus a PDF copy from your browser.
  • Photos and videos: keep the original file. Messaging apps compress them and may strip the date and location stored in the file.
  • File names that sort themselves: year, month, day and what it is, for example 2026-11-09_listing.pdf. One folder per case.
  • A timeline: date, what happened and which evidence shows it. It is the first thing we will ask for.
  • In the cloud, with two-step verification, and a second copy somewhere else.

Protect your data when you share evidence

  • Share only what the case needs.
  • On a copy, never the original, cover the full card number (leave the last 4 digits) and other people’s details, especially those of minors.
  • Never send passwords, verification codes or bank PINs, to IHA or anyone else. No serious company asks for them.
  • Do not post your evidence on social media: it exposes your data and can weaken your case.
  • With IHA, you decide what to send. We use your data only to guide you and reply to you, and you can ask us to delete it (Colombian Law 1581 of 2012).

Was your data properly protected? Check

Any company that holds your data must protect it and use it only for what you authorised. Check these signs and these rights:

  • Warning signs: messages or calls from companies you do not know, sign-in alerts you did not trigger, strange charges or an email from the company about a breach.
  • Ask the company what data it holds about you, where it got it and what it uses it for. It must answer within 10 business days, extendable by 5 (Colombian Law 1581 of 2012, art. 14).
  • Ask for it to be corrected or deleted. It must answer within 15 business days, extendable by 8 (art. 15).
  • If it does not answer or refuses, file a complaint with the SIC, which oversees data protection (art. 16).
  • If your data was leaked: change your passwords, turn on two-step verification and alert your bank if financial data is involved. The company must report security incidents to the SIC (art. 17).

If you are a foreigner or the company is outside Colombia

Colombian law protects you all the same: if you bought or received the service in Colombia, you have the same rights as a Colombian. If the company offers its product or service in Colombia, Colombian law and the SIC may apply even if it is based abroad. These bodies can also help:

  • Superintendence of Industry and Commerce (SIC), Colombia
    Handles consumer complaints and claims, including from foreigners. sic.gov.co
  • econsumer.gov
    To report problems with cross-border online purchases. It is shared by more than 35 consumer authorities in the ICPEN network, which Colombia joined in 2013. It does not resolve individual cases, but it helps spot repeat offenders. econsumer.gov
  • European Consumer Centres (ECC-Net)
    Free help if you live in the EU, Norway or Iceland and the company is in another of those countries, for example a platform’s European subsidiary. They cannot help if the company is outside Europe. eccnet.eu
  • The bank that issued your card
    If you paid by card, you can request a chargeback under your bank’s rules. Deadlines are usually short: act quickly and with your evidence.
  • Your consulate or embassy
    Guidance, lists of lawyers and help in emergencies. It does not represent you in your complaint.
  • The OECD National Contact Point
    If the company is a multinational. See below what it is and how it works.

The OECD National Contact Point (NCP)

Colombia adheres to the OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises on Responsible Business Conduct. Every country that adopts them has a National Contact Point; Colombia’s has operated within the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Tourism since 2012 (Decree 1400 of 2012).

What it is for

It receives complaints, called “specific instances”, about a multinational’s conduct that may breach the Guidelines, including their chapter on consumer interests: truthful information, fair business practices, ways to complain and data protection. It offers good offices and free mediation between you and the company.

What it is not

It is not a court and does not replace the SIC. It does not order refunds or fines, and its decisions are not binding. If there is no agreement, it publishes a final statement with recommendations to the company.

Why it can matter in your case

  • It reaches the parent company when the local subsidiary does not answer.
  • The company receives your complaint and, before Colombia’s NCP, has 20 business days to reply.
  • Mediation is neutral, confidential and free.
  • A public statement weighs on a multinational’s reputation, so companies often prefer an agreement.
  • It works for consumer cases: in 2025, Ireland’s NCP received an individual’s complaint against the Temu platform, and the case ended in a settlement with payment in full.
  • It can run alongside other routes: the NCP asks you to report ongoing proceedings, but their existence does not prevent you from filing.

When it makes sense

  • The company is a multinational or part of a group based in a country that adheres to the Guidelines.
  • The problem shows a pattern of conduct by the company, not just a price disagreement.
  • You have already complained in writing without a satisfactory answer.

Where to file: with Colombia’s NCP if the conduct took place in Colombia, or with the NCP of the country where the company is based. The OECD publishes the list of NCPs of the more than 50 adhering countries.

How it works in Colombia

  1. A written complaint in Spanish, with the facts and their evidence, the Guidelines allegedly breached, what you ask of the company and any other ongoing proceedings.
  2. The NCP forwards it to the company, which has 20 business days to reply.
  3. Initial assessment: up to two months to accept or reject the case.
  4. Good offices and mediation, voluntary and confidential.
  5. Agreement or final statement. The indicative total timeframe is one year.

Here evidence decides everything: the NCP accepts or rejects the case depending on how well the complaint is substantiated, and your evidence is what shows the link between the company’s conduct and the harm you suffered.

Can we represent you?

Yes. Colombia’s NCP accepts a complaint filed on someone else’s behalf, with that person’s express written authorisation. Our lawyers can prepare and file the specific instance with your authorisation and accompany you in the mediation. The NCPs of other countries have their own rules; we check them before filing.
