Renting out a finca or a house in a small town? What changes outside Medellín

What the law says, the deadlines and where to complain. Then get free guidance in 2 minutes.

Reference translation of Colombian rules: the Spanish legal text prevails.

Information updated on 19 November 2026.

Deadlines that matter

  • Get the land-use statement before buying, renting or investing in improvements.
  • RNT: before your first guest; renew it every year between January and March.
  • FONTUR: every quarter. ICA: on the dates of the tax calendar of the finca’s municipality.

What the law says

  • The national rules are the same as in Medellín: an RNT before you start, renewed every year; the FONTUR contribution every quarter; liability insurance for guests and third parties (Law 2068 of 2020, art. 32); guest registration (TRA, plus SIRE for foreigners); and a code of conduct against the sexual exploitation of minors.
  • What changes is land use. Medellín has a POT that says where it is allowed; most small towns have a simpler scheme (EOT) or a basic plan (PBOT) that is less precise, and almost all fincas are on rural or suburban land. A use the plan does not list is prohibited (Decree 3600 of 2007, art. 9, compiled in Decree 1077 of 2015).
  • If the finca is in a subdivision or a country condominium under horizontal property, the rule is the same as in a building: the registered rules must expressly authorise tourist accommodation (Law 675 of 2001; SIC opinion 25-585550 of 2026). A standalone finca needs no assembly approval.
  • Water: if the finca takes water from a spring or a stream, it needs a water concession from the regional environmental authority (in Antioquia, Cornare, Corantioquia or Corpourabá depending on the area), listing each use, including the pool. Wastewater: a septic tank for a dispersed rural home built to the technical standard (RAS) needs no discharge permit (Law 1955 of 2019, art. 279); if you host large groups, ask the authority in writing whether your system needs one.
  • Pool: if you offer it to your guests, treat it as a collective-use pool, because the law only considers a single-family home’s pool “private”. It must have a fence with access control, an immersion alarm, a first-aid kit, two ring buoys with rope, marked depth, an emergency phone and a lifeguard. Fines range from 50 to 1,000 monthly minimum wages (Law 1209 of 2008, arts. 4, 11, 14 and 16).
  • Glamping: a light, removable structure may not need a building permit, but only if its main use is not housing people (MinVivienda opinion 2022EE0051947). If guests sleep in it, plan on a permit, and in any case on land use that allows it.
  • Local taxes: ICA is paid in the municipality where the service is provided, that is, where the finca is, even if you live in Medellín (Law 1819 of 2016, art. 343). The rate and the fire-service surcharge are set by each municipality’s tax code.
  • Heritage: being a “Heritage Town” (Jardín, Jericó, Santa Fe de Antioquia) imposes no obligations on you. But if the property is a national cultural heritage asset, lies in its zone of influence or in a historic sector, any works that could affect it need prior authorisation from the Ministry of Culture (Law 397 of 1997, amended by Law 1185 of 2008).

Where and how to complain

  1. Land use and permits: the Planning office of the finca’s municipality.
  2. Water and wastewater: the regional environmental authority for the area.
  3. Pool: the office the municipality designates to certify safety (Law 1209 of 2008, art. 10).
  4. ICA: the Treasury office of the finca’s municipality.

Documents to gather

  • Property title certificate
  • Land-use statement from the Planning office
  • Horizontal property rules, if it is a subdivision or condominium
  • Water concession resolution
  • Pool safety certificate
  • RNT and liability insurance policy
