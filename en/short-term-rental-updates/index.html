What could change for short-term rentals

A draft decree and Medellín’s new zoning plan (POT) could change the rules for hosts and platforms, and artificial intelligence has already changed how your listings appear. What is still a draft is not law yet: this is what is known as of 13 November 2026.

Draft decree on the National Tourism Registry (MinCIT)

Status: Draft. The previous government reopened comments in July 2026. The published text would take effect on 1 January 2028; we know of no decision by the current government on whether to issue it.

  • One RNT per property: a single registration for a building or portfolio would no longer be valid.
  • In a condominium, proof that the building rules allow short-term rentals (the rules or a certificate from the administrator).
  • If the operator is not the owner, a sworn statement of the owner’s permission.
  • A valid RNT number visible on every listing.
  • Platforms would have to register in the RNT and remove listings without a valid RNT or with mismatched details, checking every six months.
  • Platforms would have six months from entry into force to connect to the RNT.

Sources: MinCIT, borrador del decreto del Registro Nacional de Turismo (2026)

Draft new zoning plan (POT) for Medellín

Status: Draft under discussion (September 2026).

  • It would restrict the areas where short-term rentals are allowed and ban them in large residential areas.
  • According to the trade group Asohost, it sets no transition period and “established use” would not protect those operating today in residential areas.

Sources: Portafolio, 11 sep. 2026

Artificial intelligence on accommodation platforms

Status: Already in use. Airbnb announced it on 20 May and 30 September 2026; some features start in the United States.

  • The platform uses AI to summarise each listing’s reviews: a recurring complaint stays visible even if the latest reviews are good.
  • Search results show AI-written descriptions highlighting each home’s key features. Check that they match reality: specific conditions that are advertised are binding (Colombian Law 1480 of 2011, art. 29).
  • AI chat support now works in more than 50 languages. Keep your conversations: if you are misinformed, they are your evidence.

Sources: Airbnb, actualización de otoño (30 sep. 2026)

What to do now

  • Keep a valid RNT for each unit and renew it before 31 March.
  • Gather the building rules or a certificate from the administrator now.
  • If you manage other owners’ properties, get each owner’s written permission.
  • Keep guest records (TRA) and SIRE reporting up to date.
  • Read your listing as guests see it, including AI-written texts and summaries, and ask for anything untrue to be corrected.

Review: is your rental compliant?

  • RNT: active, in the name of whoever operates, and shown on every listing.
  • Building (condo) rules or the administrator’s certificate: whether short-term rentals are allowed.
  • The owner’s written authorisation, if you are not the owner.
  • Guest records (TRA and SIRE) and the FONTUR contribution.
  • Your listing as guests see it, including AI-written texts: that it promises nothing the home lacks.
  • A written report listing what is missing, by urgency, and what would change if the RNT decree is issued.
