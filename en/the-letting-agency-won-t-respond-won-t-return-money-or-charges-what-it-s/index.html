Your rights and obligations in Colombia › Renting and housing

The letting agency won’t respond, won’t return money or charges what it shouldn’t

Information updated on 8 November 2026.

Deadlines that matter

  • Written complaint to the agency first: keep proof of sending.
  • Complaint to the city hall: file it with the lease and receipts.

What the law says

  • Anyone whose business is renting out or brokering urban housing in municipalities of more than 15,000 inhabitants must be registered with the local authority (Law 820 of 2003, art. 28).
  • The city hall (in Bogotá, the Housing Secretariat) supervises landlords and agencies and handles complaints, for example about unlawful deposits or failure to provide a copy of the lease or receipts (Law 820 of 2003, art. 33).
  • It can impose fines of up to 100 minimum monthly wages and suspend or cancel the registration (Law 820 of 2003, art. 34).
  • You cannot be required to pay cash deposits or give other real guarantees to secure payment (Law 820 of 2003, art. 16).

Where and how to complain

  1. Ask in writing for a copy of the lease, the receipts and a statement of what you are owed.
  2. Check with the city hall whether the agency is registered.
  3. File a complaint with the city hall (in Bogotá, the Housing Secretariat).
  4. If money is in dispute, conciliation and, failing agreement, a civil judge.

Documents to gather

  • Lease and annexes
  • Receipts and payment statements
  • Emails and messages with the agency
  • Proof of the written complaint
