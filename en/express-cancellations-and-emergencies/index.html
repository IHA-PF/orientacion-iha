Accommodation apps

For example Airbnb or Booking, stays under 30 days

  • My booking was cancelled · Your deadlines
    Legal basis: consumer, contract and criminal law

    Contract route (civil and commercial)

    • Commercial Code, arts. 1192 et seq.: accommodation provided by a business in that trade is a commercial contract.
    • Commercial Code, art. 870: in a commercial contract, if the other party defaults, you can seek termination with damages or performance with damages for the delay.
    • Civil Code, arts. 1613 and 1614: damages cover actual loss (what you lost or overpaid) and lost profit (what you failed to earn).
    • Law 1480 of 2011, art. 58(3): a claim before the SIC must be filed within one year of the end of the guarantee, the end of the contract or, in other cases, of when you learned of the facts.
    • Law 2068 of 2020, arts. 38 and 39: a tourism platform is itself a tourism provider, must hold a valid RNT and remove providers without one; it is liable to you for misleading advertising if it lets providers without an active RNT advertise.
    • Law 1480 of 2011, art. 53: the platform must have the provider identified (name, ID, address and phone) and give you those details so you can claim.
    • Civil Code, art. 1602: a contract is binding law between the parties; the terms you accepted also bind the platform.

    Criminal route

    • A breach of contract is not, by itself, a crime: it is pursued through consumer or contract law. There is a crime when there was deception from the start, for example a fake listing, shop or seller.
    • Criminal Code, art. 246, fraud (estafa): obtaining an unlawful gain by deceiving another person. 32 to 144 months in prison; 16 to 36 months if the amount is no more than 10 minimum monthly wages (about COP 17.5 million in 2026).
    • Law 906 of 2004, arts. 73 and 74: fraud and simple theft of up to 150 minimum wages (about COP 262 million in 2026) require a complaint by the victim (querella) within 6 months of the events, with exceptions such as being caught in the act. Computer crimes (arts. 269A to 269J) and larger frauds are prosecuted ex officio: a report is enough.

    Police and your home

    • Constitution, art. 28, and Constitutional Court, C-519 of 2007: a hotel room or temporary accommodation is a home; it can only be searched with a written order from a competent judicial authority.
    • Law 1801 of 2016, art. 163: the police may only enter a property without a written order in cases of pressing necessity: helping someone who asks, a fire or flood, a dangerous animal, an intrusion into an absent person’s home, attacks from inside or fireworks. The Constitutional Court (C-212 of 2017) upheld it subject to later judicial review: you can ask the guarantees judge to review the entry.
    • Law 1801 of 2016, art. 80: police protection of occupation of a property is provisional and does not decide rights, which a judge resolves; it expires after 4 months.
    • Law 1801 of 2016, art. 21: any police procedure may be recorded; no one may prevent it without an express legal restriction.

    Criminal route

    • Criminal Code, art. 182, unlawful coercion: forcing someone to do, tolerate or refrain from something. 16 to 36 months in prison.
    • Criminal Code, art. 189, violation of another’s dwelling: entering someone’s accommodation without permission, or listening to, recording or filming their private life inside. A fine; if committed by a public official, also loss of office (art. 190).
    • Criminal Code, art. 134A, acts of discrimination: preventing or restricting a person’s rights because of race, nationality, sex, sexual orientation, disability or other grounds. 12 to 36 months in prison and a fine of 10 to 15 minimum wages.
    • Where to report: the Attorney General’s Office (Fiscalía) or the National Police CAI Virtual. Keep the report number: the bank or platform may ask for it.

Lease of 30 days or more, with the owner or a letting agency

  • My landlord wants me out, changed the lock or cut off utilities · Your deadlines
    Legal basis: consumer, contract and criminal law

    Contract route (civil and commercial)

    • Law 820 of 2003, art. 22: the landlord ends the lease on statutory grounds; without fault by the tenant, during renewals, with 3 months’ written notice and compensation of 3 months’ rent (para. 7).
    • Law 820 of 2003, art. 24: the tenant can end the lease if the landlord deliberately cuts off utilities (para. 1), or at expiry with 3 months’ notice and no compensation (para. 5).
    • General Code of Procedure, art. 384: repossession of a rented property is ordered by a judge; if the claim is for non-payment, the tenant must deposit what is owed and the ongoing rent to be heard.
    • Civil Code, arts. 1613 and 1614: damages cover actual loss (what you lost or overpaid) and lost profit (what you failed to earn).
    • Decree 1423 of 2026 (earthquake of 10 August 2026): for directly affected tenants, it suspends enforcement of repossessions for 6 months, postpones rent increases and allows 6-month extensions of leases ending between 10 August and 31 December 2026.

    Police and your home

    • Law 1801 of 2016, arts. 77(5) and 79, and Civil Code, art. 775: preventing a lawful holder from entering or using a property breaches the coexistence rules; the tenant, as a holder, can ask the inspector to restore occupation (Constitutional Court, T-164 of 2024).
    • Law 1801 of 2016, art. 80: police protection of occupation of a property is provisional and does not decide rights, which a judge resolves; it expires after 4 months.

Flights and agencies

Airlines and online travel agencies

  • Flight cancelled or denied boarding (overbooking) · Your deadlines
    Legal basis: consumer, contract and criminal law

    Contract route (civil and commercial)

    • Commercial Code, arts. 981 and 982: the carrier must take you and your baggage to the destination, within the agreed time and by the agreed means, and carry you safe and sound.
    • RAC 3, para. 3.10.2.13.1: if the flight is cancelled or delayed by force majeure or weather, the airline is released by refunding the fare without penalty, and you can ask for an immediate refund.
    • Commercial Code, art. 1882: if the journey cannot start or is delayed by force majeure or weather, the airline is released by refunding the fare; if it is interrupted en route, it must carry you to your destination at its own cost and pay reasonable meals and lodging.
    • Andean Community Decision 619, for flights departing Bolivia, Colombia, Ecuador or Peru: on cancellation, the next flight, alternative transport or a full refund without penalty (art. 7), paid within 5 business days (art. 11); for overbooking or a delay of more than 6 hours attributable to the airline, minimum compensation of 25% of the fare (art. 8).
    • Civil Code, arts. 1613 and 1614: damages cover actual loss (what you lost or overpaid) and lost profit (what you failed to earn).
    • Commercial Code, art. 993: actions under a contract of carriage are time-barred 2 years after the journey ended or should have ended.

    Criminal route

    • A cancelled or delayed flight is not a criminal matter: you claim from the airline, before the Superintendency of Transport or through contract law.

    • Commercial Code, art. 1882: if the journey cannot start or is delayed by force majeure or weather, the airline is released by refunding the fare; if it is interrupted en route, it must carry you to your destination at its own cost and pay reasonable meals and lodging.

    • Montreal Convention 1999 (Colombian Law 701 of 2001), international flights: the airline is liable for delay (art. 19) and baggage (art. 17), within the limits of art. 22; claims must be filed within 2 years (art. 35).
    • Civil Code, arts. 1613 and 1614: damages cover actual loss (what you lost or overpaid) and lost profit (what you failed to earn).

Hosts and sellers

A guest who won’t leave, your account, payouts or listing

  • A guest won’t leave or breaks the rules: can I call the police? · Your deadlines
    Legal basis: consumer, contract and criminal law

    Contract route (civil and commercial)

    • Law 300 of 1996, art. 79 (amended by Law 2068 of 2020): accommodation is a standard-form contract to lodge a person for less than 30 days for a price.
    • Commercial Code, art. 1197: accommodation ends when the term expires, with 12 hours’ notice if there is no term, for non-payment, breach of the rules or another cause expressly agreed.
    • Commercial Code, art. 1198: if the provider ends the contract, it makes an inventory of the guest’s belongings in front of witnesses and may remove them.
    • Commercial Code, art. 1199: if the guest doesn’t pay, the provider may have the guest’s belongings auctioned by an authorised auctioneer and be paid from the proceeds; any surplus is held for the guest.
    • Law 300 of 1996, art. 66: if the stay cannot be extended, the provider suspends the service and lets the guest collect their belongings or places them in safe storage.

    Police and your home

    • Law 1801 of 2016, arts. 77, 79 and 80: the lawful possessor or holder can file a complaint with the inspector when prevented from using the property; the measure is provisional and lapses after 4 months. The 48-hour preventive action (art. 81) is meant for unlawful occupations.
    • Constitution, art. 28, and Constitutional Court, C-519 of 2007: a hotel room or temporary accommodation is a home; it can only be searched with a written order from a competent judicial authority.
    • Law 1801 of 2016, art. 33: noise from parties or gatherings that disturbs the neighbourhood breaches the coexistence rules; the police can intervene.

    Criminal route

    • Criminal Code, art. 189, violation of another’s dwelling: entering someone’s accommodation without permission, or listening to, recording or filming their private life inside. A fine; if committed by a public official, also loss of office (art. 190).
    • Criminal Code, art. 182, unlawful coercion: forcing someone to do, tolerate or refrain from something. 16 to 36 months in prison.
    • Criminal Code, art. 134A, acts of discrimination: preventing or restricting a person’s rights because of race, nationality, sex, sexual orientation, disability or other grounds. 12 to 36 months in prison and a fine of 10 to 15 minimum wages.
